Section 03 · Communication Reset

The Loop-Interruption Scripts

Phrases that work in the loop window — and ones to retire entirely until this intervention is solid.

Say this  ·  not this
“I’m not feeding that thought or worry.”
instead of “You’re still thinking about that?”
“It is in the past — we can let it go.”
instead of repeating the same explanation a fifth time
“Tell me what’s happening right now.”
instead of “Why are you still upset about that?”
“That detective brain is working hard. Want to put it in the folder?”
instead of “Just stop thinking about it.” (Never use STOP.)
“You came back faster today than last week.”
instead of a generic “good job!”
The single most important rule
Don't argue with the content of the loop. Jamie already KNOWS rationally. The work is co-regulating the emotional brain that's stuck — and that brain doesn't speak in explanations. It speaks in tone, presence, hugs, and short repeated scripts.

Section 04 · The 4-Week Plan

Your Implementation Map

Each week has one primary lever. Skipping ahead almost always backfires; the foundation weeks are doing real work even when it doesn't feel like it.

Week 1 · Observe & stabilize
Install the loop-interruption language. The whole household uses the same scripts every time. No new strategies yet — just the floor.

Track ONE data point: how many minutes did the longest loop last today? You're looking for a downward trend, not zero.

Expect: he will probably loop. The point isn't to extinguish it in week 1 — it's to install the response so it doesn't escalate.
Week 2 · Targeted intervention
Externalize the loop. Give the looping thought a structured place to go — out of his head, onto paper. Introduce a literal physical folder he can draw or write into, then close.

Expect: the folder may feel silly the first time. Stay calm; offer again next time the loop shows up.

Weeks 3 and 4 carry the same structure — one lever, the actions for that week, and what to expect while it beds in.

Section 01 · Issue Summary

What's Actually Happening

Jamie is perseverating on an isolated, weeks-old social incident at karate. The actual incident is closed. The loop is what remains. He already knows rationally that it's over — but his brain is replaying it the way it replays everything that touched the deeper wound.

The collision points

An isolated incident touched a deeper trauma narrative. The moment is small in itself. What it activated is large. The work isn't the incident; it's the loop the incident now lives inside.
Three triggers stacked at once. The word “STOP.” Fear of his attachment objects being damaged. Overwhelm at not being able to play socially. Any one alone might have been recoverable.
He KNOWS rationally — that's why explanations don't land. His thinking brain has all the right information. It's the emotional brain that's stuck.
The leading indicator is good. Loops are shortening. He's letting go faster than three weeks ago. Frequency drops after duration drops.
Reframe
This report is not about closing the incident — it's already closed. It's about teaching the loop that it doesn't have to keep repeating, and protecting his social re-entry.
