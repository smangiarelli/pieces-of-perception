Service Provider / Therapy Guide

The Family–Provider Handshake

Clinical work translates to lived skill when home and clinic use the same vocabulary, the same tools, and the same definition of progress.

What home is doing  ·  how session builds on it
Home: honor every hug request, on demand.
Session: same. Hugs in session are regulating, not boundary violations — when offered and consented to.
Home: “I’m not going to feed that thought” is the stuck-thought script.
Session: mirror exactly. Don’t engage trick-question content. Acknowledge the feeling, redirect to interest or body.
Home: “STOP” is retired across the household.
Session: same — use pause / wait / hold on / freeze.
Home: get to his eye level when he’s stretched.
Session: same. Physical-height parity is grounding for him.

The guide gives your provider what no intake form ever asks — client profile, regulation tools, tuning your session, and aligning with home.

Sample
Teacher / School Staff Guide

Recommended Supports

Neuro-affirming, and tuned for the group settings your child actually attends.

Environmental

Soft or lamp-light over fluorescent where possible. A predictable seat — the same one each session.
An exit option that is never penalized — a pre-agreed card or signal he can use to step out for three minutes.

Instructional

Pre-brief before each session: who, what, how long. First/Then framing for non-preferred tasks.
Directions broken into one step, with 5–10 second processing pauses. Special interests as on-ramps.

Communication

Eye contact NOT required to demonstrate listening. Stims treated as regulation, not behavior to extinguish.
The word “STOP” is retired. Honor self-advocacy: “I need a break” → break, no negotiation.

Babysitter Care Guide

Words That Work

“This is the single most useful section. Don't memorize — just keep this page open.”

Say this  ·  not this
“I’m not going to feed that thought or worry.”
instead of “That’s silly” / “That’s not real.”
“Pause / Wait / Hold on.”
instead of “STOP.”
“Want a hug?” — offer it; don’t make him earn it
instead of “Use your words.”
“I see you. Take a breath with me.”
instead of “Calm down.”
Calm tone, soft volume, get to his eye level
instead of a frustrated or angry expression

Extended Family & Close Friends

The Roles Only You Can Play

The safe harbor
Mom and Dad are managing the daily storm. You can be the place where Jamie goes when no demands are being made of him. That alone is gold.
The interest-keeper
Send him a book about trains. A printout of an unusual font. A postcard with a picture of an animal. The thread of being SEEN by you matters.
The patient adult
Of all the adults in his life, you may be the one who can move the slowest. That is a superpower for him.
The trauma-informed presence
Knowing that Jamie carries PTSD from school changes everything. Adults who don't push, don't startle, don't pressure — heal him just by being themselves.
